## 1. Pre-registration — written before a yard moved

### 1.1 The hole: 2, not 4, and the argument is arithmetic

The review named 4 (The Load-Bearing Palm, 0.25/75) the candidate — and in
the same paragraph ordered The Stand's corpse read first. I read it, and it
rules the Palm OUT: the Stand ran the exact prescribed fix (lengthen until
the careful leave loses its owned fit) on the same shape and stalled at
0.23 → 0.26/70, twice short. The Palm's disease is finding 1 (CHANGES-7 §7):
at 355 yards every route leaves a convertible number — Bomb leaves 78, the
lay leaves 185, and post-depth planners cash both. For the lay to concede a
whole shot CLASS the hole must be long enough that laying short of the
hazard leaves more than ~230 — the evaluator's honest two-shot cliff, the
thing that makes Rivermouth's First Braid (405 yds, 0.60/70) the best
front-four hole in the pool. No front-four Palmetto hole can reach that
cliff at its current yardage, and 4 can't get there without silly geometry.
So the rebuild goes to the slot that can carry the yards and already owns
the fiction: **hole 2, Brochure — 336 → 407, rebuilt as the cape the lagoon
always wanted to be.** Brochure is also the front four's deadest hole
(0.09/65, both its slate iterations spent) — the rebuild replaces a recorded
miss rather than risking a live "some."

The owner's confirmed favorite pattern — Rivermouth 5, The Bite, the
cape/lay-up where short is a priced POSITION — is the brief, not the clone.
The Bite is a par-5 of continuous bites and measures "some" by design (the
Ferry lesson: a cape lets every appetite buy its exact preferred bite and
converge). To make the cape REAL the bites must be **discrete and priced in
shot class**. That is what the rebuild does.

### 1.2 The design: TWO-PAGE SPREAD (par 4, 407)

The lagoon spans both pages of the brochure — two lobes and a photogenic
dry seam, cut diagonally across the second fairway. The pin line (green
offset −10) leans over the deep left lobe: the cape. Three water cells,
one lagoon in the fiction:

- **A, the shallow near-right lobe** ({down 212, side 14} r 12×22, spans
  196–216 deep, −8..+36 wide): sits ON the direct line at lay depth. The
  naked Long Iron's rest band (198–218) lives in it — the lazy long road is
  caught, not gifted (rest-judged, per the skip-across clause's water half).
- **B, the deep far-left lobe** ({down 262, side −34} r 22×26, spans
  240–284 deep, −60..−8 wide): under the pin line at exactly the Bomb's
  drawn rest band (264–290). The cape carry: the naked Bomb at the pin has
  its left band honestly shaded — drawn, a genuine fraction, no dice-doubt;
  the bail-right Bomb buys dry for a rough-taxed longer leave.
- **C, the right finger** ({down 253, side 16} r 14×14, spans 239–267 deep,
  +2..+30 wide): closes the Stinger's free thread — the low runner's
  rest (244–266) dies in it, and lowFlight pitching into A is wet by the
  pitch rule anyway. The game's namesake card drowns in the photograph.

The routes, by SHOT CLASS (the law finding 1 demands):

1. **The buy:** naked Bomb, pin line — rest 264–290 clears A (ends 216) and
   C's far edge (267) by the skip; the left band is over B. Leaves ~130 —
   the owned Short Iron, 133, exact. Aggressive's road; aim (pin vs right)
   mediates the bite.
2. **The priced position:** the tight lay — LI+Choke finishes 183, leave
   ~224, dry by drawn margin, keeps the one-shot argument at its worst
   proximity; or the naked LI threaded LEFT of A (dry only left of the
   seam, trees tail at the far edge). Short keeps arguing; short is not
   free.
3. **The concession:** Mid Iron, 170, unconditionally dry, leave ~237 —
   past the two-shot cliff. The chicken-out exists and has a posted price
   of a whole class.

Drop-shadow audit (done on paper before measuring, the Toll's lesson):
every wet pitch drops dry. A's side-0 span is 198–214, so its own drops
(≤191) and B's (215–259, B never touches side 0) and C's (214–242) all land
short of or past it. Softlock by construction: every cell is wedge-flyable
from short; from the greenside bunkerless surrounds every leave is owned.
