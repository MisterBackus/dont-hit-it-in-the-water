## 4. The Mirage — the diagnosis executed, the hole still missed, the kill is registered

### 4.1 The census (new instrument, run before touching anything)

Tee-level census at SF6, 800 rounds per appetite: **nobody plays the hole in
the file.** The naked Bomb — "fly it all" — is 22% of aggressive and 0% of
everyone else, and 35% of those Bombs pitch into the slab. Mixed's most
popular route is a 200-yard iron aimed LEFT that pitches at ~200 and never
sees the pan, which starts at 228. Two findings, both mechanical:

1. **CHANGES-7 applied the water clause to crust.** The Toll taught that
   water reads a high ball's REST band (the skip-across). Crust is the
   opposite: `rollAfterPitch` cuts the run 60% on a deep pitch, so **crust
   holds what pitches in it — a crust carry's honest picture is the PITCH
   band against the far edge, not the rest band.** At 240–270 the Bomb's
   pitch band (252–278) was two-thirds shaded, not the "quarter-shaded
   tail" the v8 comment computed. Not a tempter — a sucker punch, correctly
   declined by every appetite that could see it. **The law gains this as
   the skip-across clause's dual**, and the pair should be quoted together:
   *water reads the high ball's rest; crust reads its pitch.*
2. **The advertised "long way round" was free.** The pan priced only
   Bomb-depth left misses; the 200-yard lay-left line was never priced at
   its own pitch depth.

### 4.2 One attempt (predictions first), and the miss

v9: slab moved 12 shallower (228–258 — the far edge a true quarter into the
pitch band, computed on the right band this time); pan grown shallower
(188–312) to price the left iron. Predicted: gap 0.35–0.55, split 65–75,
mixed −0.15..+0.10, aggressive best via the Bomb.

**Measured: 0.33/65 (N=400), 0.34/67 (N=800), mixed −0.07/−0.03.** The
post-change census shows the diagnosis executed exactly: the left iron is
dead, the naked Bomb is back as aggressive's route (24% share, −0.38 mean,
18% pitch-shaded — the intended quarter), the lay and the fly are priced
apart in the intended directions. And the gap still stops one hundredth
short of the bar, because at 300 yards **every route's floor is a birdie
look** — COURSE-TODO's oldest rule ("everything asking for 150–195 measures
flat" has a 300-yard sibling: everything REACHABLE by everyone converges at
the green). The Oak survives at Rockdale on 0.35/74, itself a named
knife-edge; the transplant is running out of soil, not out of tuning.
