## The one thing to understand first — it replaces the last one

**A hole contains a decision only when reaching is in doubt.**

The aim band was real and you hit it on every hole: split went 24% → 80% on The
Ballwasher. But the score gaps did not follow, on any of the five. I tested my
own hypothesis (that water/OB were too punishing and `rough` would fix it) and it
was wrong — swapping the surface on all four new hazards moved nothing.

Then, The Ballwasher with **hazards untouched, only the length changed**:

```
  150 yards (as built)    gap 0.28
  185 yards               gap 0.49
  205 yards               gap 0.86     ← would be the best hole in the game
```

At 150 everybody reaches, so the options differ and arrive at the same place. At
205 the only card that gets there is a Long Iron at ±20, so safe play refuses it
and eats a shot while aggressive takes it and doesn't.

**Hazards do not create decisions. They price decisions the distance ladder has
already created.** That is why nine revisions across two rounds moved the score
and none of them moved the fork.

The ladder, for reference: **277 · 255 · 208 · 170 · 133 · 105 · 94 · 70 · 58 ·
55 · 35.** Every REAL hole in the game sits just past one of those numbers.
Everything asking for 150–195 measures flat, on every course, every time.

## The new rule, replacing the old bar

**Before placing a single hazard, write down what reaching the target costs:
which card, at what cone width, and what the lay-up leaves. If both answers are
comfortable, no hazard will save the hole.** Then place hazards to price the
edges of the card the player had to stretch for.

Bar unchanged: **split above 60% and gap above 0.35.**
