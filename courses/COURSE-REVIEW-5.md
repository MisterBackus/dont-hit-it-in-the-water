## 1. The diagnostic you asked for — Salt Flats is starving

Focus in hand at each tee, mixed play, against Rockdale's post-fix curve:

```
  tee            1     2     3     4     5     6     7     8
  Salt Flats   5.0   4.6   1.5   1.8   2.2   2.6   2.2   2.7
  Rockdale     5.0   4.3   3.0   2.4   3.0   3.0   2.7   3.2
  Pine Hollow  5.0   3.9   1.8   2.6   2.8   2.8   3.0   3.2
```

**Tees 3 and 4 sit at half Rockdale's number.** A putt costs 2 to 4. So the
third and fourth holes of the unfair major are played by a man who cannot afford
either branch of the fork you built for him — and they measure 0.38 and 0.31,
which is exactly what "close but not forking" looks like when the cause is money
rather than design.

Your reading was right: **a fork chain drains itself.** Whoever pays at the 1st
arrives poorer at the 2nd, and by the 3rd there is nothing left. Four
consecutive forks is a shape the focus economy cannot fund.

Where I disagree is the conclusion. You suggested this may be the identity
working, since a major measures well-playing lines. It is not, and the reason is
in the same table: **Pine Hollow starves at the 3rd too** (1.8), and its 3rd is
its weakest hole at 0.26. Starvation is not difficulty, it is the absence of
choice, and it reads the same on a major as anywhere else. The mercy hole is the
fix — you are right that the 7th is load-bearing — but it is in the wrong place.
**Salt Flats needs its funding round inside the front four, not after it.**

Cheapest version: make the 2nd the cheap one instead of the 3rd. It is already
the highest-split hole on the course (80%) and only 0.30, so it is paying for a
fork it does not deliver.

## 2. The three revisions

| | predicted | measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cottonwood 3 · Postcard** | ≥0.40, split ≥65% | **0.27 / 53%** | closer, missed |
| **Salt Flats 1 · The Crossing** | a fork | **0.10 / 62%**, mean +0.13 → **+0.40** | harder, still flat |
| **Rockdale hardening** | −1.66 → −0.9/−1.1, four REAL intact | **−1.65**, four REAL intact | no movement |

**Postcard** improved on both axes and is no longer the pair's embarrassment
(0.21 → 0.27, and the mean rose from +0.47 to +0.59). Your fallback diagnosis is
worth running: Ballwasher is at −10 offset and Postcard at +18, and if the aim
band interacts with offset then a straightened copy will say so in one run.

**The Crossing** is the instructive failure. The belt moved, the naked carry
went into doubt, and the hole got 0.27 strokes harder — and the gap went *down*.
Deepening a carry does not create a fork if every appetite responds to it the
same way, and here they all buy the same certainty. Reach in doubt is necessary,
not sufficient; the second condition — a real price on the stretch card's cone —
is what makes the appetites diverge, and the pan is 30 off the line where ±14 of
aim cannot reach it.

**The Rockdale hardening did nothing measurable to the score**, and the reason is
your own law pointing the other way. Holes 1, 6 and 7 got harder; holes 6, 7 and
8 all got *easier* in the same run (+0.13 → −0.10, +0.28 → +0.17, +0.04 → −0.30).
The shadow cuts both ways: a harder hole 1 spends focus that its own shadow does
not fall on, and everything downstream is funded by the momentum bonus instead.
**Difficulty added at the front of a course leaks out the back.** That is a
finding, not a failure, and it is worth adding to the law.
