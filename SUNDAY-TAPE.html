01 · The world

The coverage of your season

Tour broadcast, 1978. A tower camera holding on a par 4, phosphor numbers burned into the corner of the tube, a leaderboard treated like scripture. Everything on screen is something television actually put on screen: a bug, a chyron, a telestrator mark. The interface never pretends to be grass — it is always a picture of the course, which is exactly the honesty the cone needs. Dark ground, three light voices: phosphor for information, gold for money and honour, white for the cone and nothing else.

Casing rule. The cone is drawn twice: Tube Black at 4.5px underneath, Telestrator White at 2px on top — the way a real telestrator line survived any picture. This makes the cone legible over sand, and over anything.

Outline rule. Water and fairway are hue-separated but luminance-flat, so every hazard shape carries a Tube Black outline. The outline is load-bearing, not styling — it is also what keeps the map readable for colour-blind players.

03 · Typography

Two faces, no exceptions

Anton — display. Event names, card names, chyron titles, the wordmark. Always caps, tracking +3%
The Masters of Pine Hollow
IBM Plex Mono — everything else. Numerals always tabular; flavour text in italic; labels caps, tracking +12–18%
265 $41,250 Round 4 at the Sunbelt Open, and the wind is doing something personal. THRU 14 · E The head cover was a gift.

Scale: 40 / 26 / 18 / 14.5 / 11 / 10.5. No third face, no regular-width sans anywhere. If a string contains a number the player compares — yardage, score, money — it is Plex Mono with tabular-nums, so columns land, always.

04 · The hole view

The picture, the mark, the chyron

  • The plan is the picture. Rough fills the frame; fairway, sand, water and green sit on it as outlined shapes; phosphor gridlines every 50 yards with small left-edge labels. Nothing is textured — surface identity comes from colour plus outline.
  • The cone is a telestrator mark. White 2px over a 4.5px Tube Black casing, 15% white fill, dashed aim line, a white dot on the ball. It is the only white shape in the frame, so the eye cannot miss it and never has to search.
  • One bug, one chyron. Hole info top-left in a bordered phosphor bug; the leaderboard chyron rises from the bottom over its own black band, never over the tee. Nothing else may sit on the picture. When a third thing wants to exist, it replaces the chyron; it does not join it.
  • Scanlines and vignette are one layer each, at fixed recipe opacity — degradation is set dressing, and set dressing never gets louder than the play.

07 · Texture, motion, discipline

The rules that keep it honest

  • One chyron, one bug. The 1978 idiom fails by addition — tickers, double bugs, replay stingers. Everything after the first chyron is clutter.
  • Only the cone is white. Only intent glows. Text-shadow glow is permitted on exactly three things: the wordmark, focus pips, and fresh under-par scores. Never on body text.
  • Degradation is fixed, not expressive. Scanlines: 1px light / 2px dark at 5.5% / 14%, blend overlay. Vignette: one radial, 55% Tube Black at the corners. One flicker every 6 seconds, one frame long, and none at all under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Motion is a hard cut. Broadcast doesn't ease; it cuts. State changes land in steps, leaderboard rows swap with a single flip, and the ball's result arrives like a replay: cut, hold, chyron. No tweens softer than steps() — save true animation for where it earns its place, later.
  • Signal Red is spent, never decorates. Penalty strokes, the cut line, a ball in the water. If red appears when nothing is wrong, red stops meaning something is wrong.
08 · Drop-in

The theme contract

Shipped alongside this guide as sunday-tape.css. Components read tokens only — no literal colours in component styles — so the current spare look survives as a dev-toggle fallback (data-theme="yardage") and playtests can A/B the two without a rebuild.

Fourteen colour tokens and two font slots are the whole contract. A future theme is a second block of the same fourteen names — nothing in the components changes.
